# Anchor Text & Link Quality

How to judge whether a backlink is worth chasing, and how to think about anchor text — so the
plan chases links that help and skips links that waste time or cause harm.

## Is this link worth chasing? (a quick rubric)

Score a prospective link on four axes:

1. **Authority (DR / domain strength).** Higher is better, but relevance can outweigh raw DR.
2. **Relevance.** A link from a site in (or adjacent to) your niche is worth more than a
   higher-DR link from an unrelated one. Topical relevance is a strong signal.
3. **Traffic.** Will real, relevant people click it? A link that sends buyers is valuable even
   if nofollow.
4. **Effort.** A 5-minute free form vs. a multi-week outreach. Rank by payoff ÷ effort.

Chase first: **relevant + high-DR + low-effort.** Deprioritize: unrelated, low-DR, high-effort.

## dofollow vs nofollow

- **dofollow** passes link authority — the classic SEO benefit.
- **nofollow / ugc / sponsored** don't pass authority the same way, but still bring **referral
  traffic, brand and entity signals, and citations in AI search**. Modern search also treats
  nofollow as a "hint", not an absolute.
- **Verdict:** get both. Never skip a high-authority, high-traffic listing (G2, Crunchbase,
  Wikipedia-adjacent, big marketplaces) just because it's nofollow.

## Anchor text (mostly out of your hands for directories)

For directories the anchor is usually your brand/product name or bare URL — which is exactly
what you want. Across your whole backlink profile, aim for a **natural mix**, dominated by:

- **Branded** anchors ("BacklinkBot", "backlinkbot.ai") — the safe majority.
- **Naked URLs** (backlinkbot.ai).
- **Generic** ("this tool", "here").
- A **small** amount of partial/exact-match keyword anchors ("SaaS directory submission tool").

An over-optimized profile stuffed with exact-match keyword anchors looks manipulative and is a
classic penalty pattern. For listicle outreach, suggest a branded or natural phrase — never
demand an exact-match keyword anchor.

## Link patterns to avoid

- **Link farms / PBNs / "500 backlinks for $5"** — low-quality footprints that can hurt.
- **Irrelevant bulk directories** with no real audience — noise, sometimes toxic.
- **Paid dofollow links that aren't marked sponsored** — against search guidelines.
- **Exact-match anchor over-optimization** (above).
- **Reciprocal-link schemes** at scale.

## The honest bottom line

A handful of relevant, high-authority listings and a few contextual listicle links beat hundreds
of junk directory submissions. Quality and relevance over raw count. Rank the plan accordingly —
and when a directory in `data/directories.md` is low-DR *and* off-topic *and* paid, it's usually
fine to skip it.
