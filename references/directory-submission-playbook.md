# Directory Submission Playbook

How to submit a product to a directory so it actually gets **approved** and the listing pulls
its weight. Prep once, reuse everywhere.

## Prepare these assets once (reuse across every directory)

- **Name** — exact product name, spelled/capitalized consistently everywhere.
- **Tagline** — one line, ~60 characters, benefit-first ("Invoicing that pays you in a day").
- **Descriptions in two lengths:**
  - ~50 words — for tight forms.
  - ~100–160 words — for full profiles. Lead with what it does + who it's for, then 2–3
    differentiators. Plain language, no buzzwords.
- **Category** — know your primary + secondary category; pick the closest the directory offers.
- **Logo** — square (min 512×512), transparent PNG, plus a wide/banner version if available.
- **Screenshots** — 3–5 clean shots (or a 30–60s demo video for launch platforms).
- **Links** — homepage URL, and a deep link to the most relevant feature/pricing page.
- **Founder / company details** — name, role, location, founding year, team size, socials.
- **Consistent NAP** — Name, Address, Phone identical across every business listing. Google
  cross-checks these; inconsistency weakens local/business signals.

## Rules that decide approval

- **Match the category honestly.** Miscategorized listings are the #1 rejection reason.
- **Real, working links.** A dead or redirecting URL gets rejected. HTTPS, no tracking params
  that break.
- **No keyword stuffing** in the name or description. Directories reject spammy copy.
- **A real product page.** Make sure the site loads fast, has a clear value prop above the
  fold, and doesn't 404 on mobile — reviewers click through.
- **Unique description per directory when it's cheap to do so.** Identical boilerplate across
  50 sites is fine for low-stakes directories but reads better (and ranks better) with light
  variation on the high-value ones.

## Follow type — chase, but don't skip nofollow

- **dofollow** passes authority — prioritize these for pure SEO.
- **nofollow** (G2, Capterra, Crunchbase, most DR90+ marketplaces) still delivers **referral
  traffic, brand/entity signals, and AI-search citations**. A nofollow listing on a DR90 buyer
  platform is worth more than a dofollow on a DR20 spam directory. Get both.

## Launch platforms need more (Product Hunt, BetaList, etc.)

- A gallery (logo + 3–5 screenshots + ideally a 60s demo), a punchy tagline, and a strong
  **first comment** from the maker.
- Time it: Product Hunt resets at 12:01am PT — schedule and line up genuine early engagement.
- One good launch day > ten low-effort listings. Don't burn a launch platform on a rushed post.

## After submitting

- **Track status** (submitted → live) so you don't re-submit or lose track. Many free listings
  sit in a manual review queue for days or weeks — that's normal.
- Where the directory offers a **badge/embed**, add it to your site: it's often an extra
  backlink and a trust signal.
- Recheck high-value listings after a month — some silently expire or require reconfirmation.

## What not to do

- Don't mass-submit to low-quality link-farm directories for the sake of volume — DR90 nofollow
  beats fifty DR5 dofollow links, and link-farm patterns can hurt.
- Don't use fake or inconsistent business details across listings.
- Don't pay to skip queues on directories that send you no relevant traffic — spend effort
  where the audience actually is.
