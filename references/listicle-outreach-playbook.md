# Listicle Outreach Playbook

Getting added to "best `<category>` tools" roundups that already rank is one of the highest-ROI
backlinks available: the page is already indexed and trusted, and one edit adds a contextual,
often-dofollow link plus real referral traffic from buyers.

## Step 1 — Find the roundups

Run these searches (swap `<category>` for the product's niche — see `data/listicle-queries.md`
for the full recipe list):

- `best <category> tools`
- `top <category> software`
- `<category> tools 2026`
- `best <competitor> alternatives`
- `<category> tools for <audience>` (e.g. "for startups", "for agencies")
- `intitle:"best <category>"`

Prioritize results that:
- rank on page 1 (already have authority),
- were **published or updated recently** (maintained = editable),
- **list tools like yours** (you fit the set),
- are on independent blogs/media (easier to edit than a competitor's own comparison page).

Skip a competitor's own "X vs Y" pages — they won't add you.

## Step 2 — Qualify the target

For each candidate note: the site/author, how entries are formatted (name + blurb + link?),
how many entries, and whether the product genuinely belongs. Only pitch where the fit is real —
a mismatched pitch just burns the relationship.

## Step 3 — Find the contact

In order of preference: a listed author email → a site contact/tip form → the author on
X/LinkedIn → a general editorial/hello@ address. Many "best of" posts explicitly invite
submissions in a footer or sidebar — use that route first.

## Step 4 — Write the pitch

Keep it short, specific, and human. A working structure:

1. **One genuine, specific line** about the actual post (reference a real detail — proves you
   read it, not scraped it).
2. **One line** on what the product is and the single reason it fits this list (the gap it
   fills).
3. **A paste-ready entry** in the post's own format: product name, a one-sentence blurb, and
   the URL — so the author can add it in ten seconds.
4. **A soft ask:** "Would you consider adding it?" — no pressure, no fake deadline.

Avoid: "I hope this email finds you well", flattery padding, "we're the #1 best leading
solution", fake urgency, or attaching a media kit. Offer a reciprocal share or a quick quote/
stat they can use if it's natural — editors like low-effort value.

## Step 5 — Follow up once

One polite follow-up after ~5–7 business days if there's no reply. Then stop. Move to the next
target — there are always more roundups than hours.

## Reality check

Reply rates are modest (think 5–15%). It's a numbers game: a good session finds 15–30 targets,
pitches the 8–12 best-fit ones, and lands a couple. Those couple are contextual, high-trust
links that a directory listing can't match.
